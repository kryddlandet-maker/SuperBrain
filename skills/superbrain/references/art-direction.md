# Art direction with freedom

Describe a direction in concrete choices: type roles, base material, dominant composition, asset treatment, accent behavior and interaction character. 'Clean and modern' does not resolve these choices.

Examples of distinct directions, not universal defaults:
- Precise tech: charcoal field, oversized grotesk display, fine orbital lines, one warm signal color, cursor-controlled product fragments.
- Salon editorial: warm ivory, expressive serif, cropped portrait photography, generous reading rhythm, quiet ink-colored booking action.
- Construction craft: mineral background, condensed display, wide real project photos, measured grid, restrained copper accents and process diagrams.

Let real content and the business's personality drive the choices. A friendly suburban salon should not inherit the visual language of enterprise software. A tech product need not be dark. Adapt, combine, or invent another direction.

Select assets before promising a composition that depends on them. Typography, crop, alignment, spacing and media provide most visual identity. Interaction can be the core concept when it explains or celebrates the product; test it early alongside static content.

Cards, gradients, pills and three-column grids remain available when useful. Their repetitive automatic use is the problem, not their existence. Judge composition through hierarchy, fit and rhythm, not a blacklist or a required number of unique decisions.

When the user wants options, present genuinely different visual hypotheses and render small studies when feasible. Otherwise recommend one direction and continue. Record a short rationale and any unresolved choices instead of producing a stack of speculative documents.
