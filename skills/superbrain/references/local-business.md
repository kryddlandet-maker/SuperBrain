# Local businesses

Visitors need to know what is offered, where, by whom and how to take the next step. Make those facts easy to find while giving the business its own visual identity. Missing facts should be asked for or marked as draft content.

## Salon

Use real treatments, prices and durations where available. Team pages should use real portraits and specialties with permission. Structure a treatment page around suitability, what happens, maintenance and booking. Surface practical questions: parking, opening hours, accessibility and cancellation terms when confirmed.

Photography can carry warmth and craft. Editorial typography, beautiful crop and subtle image changes may do more than an elaborate cursor scene. Still allow expressive interaction for a fashion-led salon when requested. Booking remains stable, readable and usable on a phone.

## Construction

Explain services, geographic coverage, process and scope. Use real projects with context: type of work, constraints, materials and outcome. Claims about certification, guarantees, years of experience or customer reviews require evidence.

Support quote requests with relevant fields and honest confirmation/error handling once a backend is connected. Before integration, make the demo status explicit. Avoid decorative dashboards. A material-led palette, measured alignment, good project photos and process clarity are strong design tools.

## Shared content practice

Do not repeat the same paragraph on every route. Reuse verified facts, write route-specific explanations, and maintain one source for contact details and opening hours. Use structured data only for confirmed business facts. Do not create misleading local landing pages or invented addresses.
